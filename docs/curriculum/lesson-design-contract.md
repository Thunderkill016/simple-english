# SE Lesson Design Contract

Rules for designing SE lessons. Every rule cites the pedagogy source(s)
that support it (`docs/research/lesson-design-frameworks.md`). This is a
**contract, not a recipe**: it constrains what must be true, not the
order of stages. Any sequence that satisfies the contract and fits the
lesson type is legal.

## Rules

### Outcome

**RULE 1 — Desired result before activities.**
A lesson spec must state a specific desired result — what the learner
can do after the lesson — before stages are designed.
_Evidence: ACTFL Backward Design ("identify desired results"); BC lesson
planning (clear, realistic aims incl. concept); CELTA 4.2 (statement of
aims)._

**RULE 2 — The result is a real-world action, not a grammar point.**
Phrase the outcome as meaningful language use.
_Evidence: CEFR action-oriented approach (learner as social agent, real-
life tasks); ACTFL communicative tasks (natural functions, meaning-
making); BC aims-with-concept._

### Evidence

**RULE 3 — Acceptable evidence defined before activities.**
The spec must say what learner behavior inside the lesson counts as
evidence of progress toward the result.
_Evidence: ACTFL Backward Design ("determine acceptable evidence" —
second, before experiences)._

**RULE 4 — Completion ≠ mastery.**
Lesson completion may claim only what the in-lesson evidence shows —
practice completed, language met, responses produced. Never "mastered",
"fluent", "independent", or durable real-world ability.
_Evidence: ACTFL Can-Do framing (statements chart a continuum);
backward-design logic (evidence bounds the claim); SE honesty rule._

### Input / material

**RULE 5 — Learning content comes from sources; SE authors only glue.**
Dialogue, model sentences, target language, explanations, exercise
options and speaking frames must trace to a licensed source (`reused` or
`adapted`). SE may author navigation, instructions, translations, and
integration text — flagged as `se-authored`.
_Evidence: SE reuse-first governance; CELTA 4.4 (adapt materials with
due regard to copyright); no pedagogy source licenses invention._

**RULE 6 — Adapt material to the lesson's scope and rights.**
Trim/extract only what the desired result needs, where the license
permits, with the adaptation logged.
_Evidence: CELTA 4.4 (selection + adaptation); BC aims (everything
referable to the aim); SE source-selection rubric._

### Listening

**RULE 7 — Listening is prepared.**
Before the first listen, give context and a reason to listen (who speaks,
what happens, what to listen for). Vocabulary needed to complete the
listening task is handled before listening.
_Evidence: BC listening framework — pre-listening = motivation,
contextualization, preparation; the challenge must be listening, not
task decoding._

**RULE 8 — Each listen has a purpose; gist before detail.**
First exposure targets general understanding; subsequent exposures can
target detail and the language itself. Repeated listening is normal.
_Evidence: BC listening framework — while-listening (gist → detail);
Evergreen pattern (Listen → Comprehension Check → "listen again")._

**RULE 9 — Language work follows meaning.**
Form focus (chunks, notes) comes after the learner has engaged with the
meaning of the input.
_Evidence: BC listening framework — post-listening language work; ARC —
Clarification as a distinct element._

### Practice

**RULE 10 — Controlled before freer, when both exist.**
Restricted-use practice precedes freer production in a language-focused
beginner lesson.
_Evidence: BC lesson planning — "as a very general rule" controlled →
freer; ARC Restricted→Authentic ordering for this lesson type;
Rosenshine — guided practice before independent practice._

**RULE 11 — Freer practice transfers the language to the learner.**
Production requires the learner to use the target language for a
real-meaning purpose — e.g., their own name — not only repeat the model.
_Evidence: ACTFL communicative tasks (meaning-making); CEFR action-
orientation (agency, real purpose); Evergreen practice patterns (cloze
frames completed with own information)._

**RULE 12 — No fake interactivity.**
Where a source task needs a partner/teacher SE doesn't have, adapt to
honest solo practice (say aloud, self-check, replay) and document the
adaptation. Never present self-practice as assessed interpersonal skill.
_Evidence: ACTFL communicative tasks (interpersonal = understanding and
being understood — a partner is definitional); SE honesty rule._

### Feedback

**RULE 13 — Immediate, specific, non-overwhelming feedback.**
Check answers give timely correct/incorrect feedback without heavy
explanation; completion feedback is self-assessment in nature.
_Evidence: ACTFL Effective Feedback (specific, timely, student-friendly,
don't overwhelm; reflective feedback supports self-evaluation)._

## Rules rejected / not supported by the research

- ❌ A universal SE stage list ("every lesson = goal → audio → chunks →
  MC → produce"). No source mandates one; BC hedges, ARC permits any
  order, TTT contradicts it.
- ❌ Grammar rules before meeting the language in use (nothing mandates
  front-loading; ARC allows C in any position; listening framework puts
  language work after meaning).
- ❌ Diagnostic tasks for absolute beginners in solo lessons (TESOL TTT
  needs live diagnosis by a teacher).
- ❌ "Complete N exercises to prove the skill" — no source sets an
  exercise count; evidence rules bound the *kind* of evidence.
- ❌ Celebratory/gamified completion — nothing pedagogical requires it;
  calm self-assessment is closer to ACTFL's reflective feedback.

## What this contract does not decide

Stage order within a lesson is chosen per lesson from the outcome, the
skill targeted, the learner and the material — documented per lesson in
its stage map.
