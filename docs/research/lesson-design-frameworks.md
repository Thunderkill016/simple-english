# Lesson Design Frameworks — Source Research

Task 005R evidence base. Every entry records the official source, what it
actually says (paraphrased from the retrieved document, not from memory),
what it does **not** say, and whether it applies to Lesson 001 —
`se-a1-meet-someone`, a ~10-minute, self-directed, offline-first lesson for
an absolute beginner.

Retrieved 2026-10-03/04. Direct-fetch limitations are flagged where they
occurred (British Council pages return HTTP 403 to automated fetch; the
content below from those pages was surfaced through the official pages'
indexed text — section quotes are kept minimal and verifiable).

Two provenance systems are deliberately kept separate:

- **Content provenance** — where the text/audio/exercise material comes
  from (the source registry + block provenance already tracks this).
- **Pedagogy rationale** — why a learning stage exists. This document is
  the rationale layer. It lives in documentation, not runtime code.

## 1. ACTFL — Plan with Backward Design

- Source: ACTFL Guiding Principles for Language Learning
- URL: https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/guiding-principles-for-language-learning/plan-with-backward-design
- Retrieved: direct fetch, 2026-10-03

What it says:

- Identify **desired results** first, then **determine acceptable
  evidence**, then **plan learning experiences and instruction**. The
  order is the substance of the principle.
- Desired results may be informed by the World-Readiness Standards, the
  ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines, and the NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements.
- Learning tasks should move learners toward the target; educators have
  flexibility — multiple pathways exist.
- The three modes of communication (interpretive, interpersonal,
  presentational) should be embedded.

What it does NOT say:

- No fixed stage list, no mandated order of activities. Backward design
  governs _planning logic_ (outcome → evidence → experiences), not a
  classroom recipe.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES — planning frame.** The lesson spec must
declare a desired result and acceptable lesson-level evidence before
activity selection is justified.

## 2. ACTFL — Design Communicative Tasks

- URL: https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/guiding-principles-for-language-learning/design-communicative-tasks
- Retrieved: direct fetch, 2026-10-03

What it says:

- Oral interpersonal communication means exchanging information and
  ideas, meeting needs, expressing/supporting opinions — learners create
  and convey meaning while **understanding and being understood**.
- Tasks should encourage cognitive engagement and linguistic
  risk-taking; emphasize meaning-making over accuracy; use natural
  language functions; be authentic and meaningful; align with
  proficiency outcomes.
- Assessment should consider comprehension and comprehensibility
  holistically.

What it does NOT say:

- Does not define how a _solo_ software product should emulate a live
  interlocutor. Interpersonal communication presumes a partner.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES, with an honest limitation.** A local app
without a partner or speech evaluation cannot deliver a genuine
interpersonal task. It can offer meaningful self-practice toward the
goal — and must not label that as interpersonal proficiency.

## 3. ACTFL — Provide Effective Feedback

- URL: https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/guiding-principles-for-language-learning/provide-effective-feedback
- Retrieved: direct fetch, 2026-10-03

What it says:

- Feedback should be specific, timely, relevant, descriptive and
  student-friendly; it comes in formative, summative and
  reflective/self-assessment forms; avoid overwhelming learners.
- Formative feedback supports progress toward targets; reflective
  feedback supports learner self-evaluation.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES.** Keeps exercise feedback immediate,
minimal and non-judgmental; justifies a self-assessment-style completion
panel rather than a score.

## 4. ACTFL — NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements

- URL: https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/ncssfl-actfl-can-do-statements
- Retrieved: direct fetch, 2026-10-03

What it says:

- Can-Do statements help learners set goals and chart progress, and help
  educators write learning targets; organized by Interpretive,
  Interpersonal, Presentational modes; they describe a continuum of
  proficiency development.

What it does NOT say / limits:

- A single successful lesson attempt is not evidence of durable mastery.
- Descriptor text is a copyrighted assessment instrument — SE does not
  copy descriptors verbatim. SE's own capability wording must be marked
  SE-authored and not presented as an official descriptor.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES — framing + honesty boundary.** Completion
language must describe lesson-level practice evidence, not proficiency.

## 5. British Council — A Framework for Planning a Listening Skills Lesson

- Source: TeachingEnglish article (BC framework draws on standard ELT
  listening pedagogy; corroborating stage language also appears in
  Pearson's "How to Teach Listening" sample material)
- URL: https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/en/professional-development/teachers/planning-lessons-and-courses/articles/framework-planning
- Retrieved: 2026-10-04 via official indexed page text (direct fetch 403)

What it says:

- **Pre-listening**: prepare learners — motivation, contextualization,
  preparation. Language/knowledge needed to _complete the task_ should
  be handled before listening so the challenge is listening, not
  decoding the task.
- **While-listening**: tasks that focus attention on the text and guide
  understanding — general/gist understanding first, then more detailed
  understanding.
- **Post-listening**: integrate what was learned with existing
  knowledge — this is where follow-on language work and speaking
  practice happen, after meaning.
- Repeated listening is normal; each listen should have a task/purpose.

What it does NOT say:

- Does not mandate that transcripts be hidden or shown at a fixed point;
  transcript policy is a design decision derived from the gist-before-
  detail principle (reading the transcript on first listen removes the
  listening task's purpose).

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES — governs the listening spine.**

## 6. British Council — Lesson Planning (aims, stages, practice types)

- Sources:
  - "The elements of a lesson plan" —
    https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/planning-lessons-and-courses/elements-lesson-plan
  - "The basics of lesson planning" (ESA: Engage–Study–Activate) —
    https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/planning-lessons-and-courses/basics-lesson-planning
  - "Lesson plans" FAQ —
    https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/planning-lessons-and-courses/lesson-plans
  - "Controlled practice" / "Free practice" — Teaching Knowledge Database
- Retrieved: 2026-10-04 via official indexed page text (direct fetch 403)

What it says:

- Plans need **clear and realistic aims**; writing aims that include the
  _concept_ (the meaning the target language carries in natural use)
  keeps planning focused and prevents confusion.
- Lessons divide into **coherent stages**, each with its own aim,
  building toward the outcome.
- Ordering guidance is deliberately soft: _"as a very general rule"_ —
  introduction → new language → controlled practice → freer practice →
  review/feedback. ESA elements are "useful to keep in mind," not a law.
- Controlled practice = restricted production of the target language;
  free practice = learners produce language freely; both can be
  meaningful; younger/lower-level learners get more controlled practice.

What it does NOT say:

- No universal mandatory sequence. "Very general rule" language
  throughout.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES — supports controlled→freer progression
and aims-with-concept, without licensing a rigid template.**

## 7. British Council — ARC (Authentic / Restricted / Clarification)

- Source: Teaching Knowledge Database, "ARC" + related entries
- URL: https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/teaching-knowledge-database/c/arc
- Retrieved: 2026-10-04 via official indexed page text (direct fetch 403)

What it says:

- **A**uthentic use — real/meaningful language use;
  **R**estricted use — controlled practice focused on form/accuracy;
  **C**larification and focus — drawing attention to form and rules.
- The three elements "can be in any order" — ARC, CRA, RAC are all
  valid; it is a labeling model for describing lesson stages, not a
  fixed sequence.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES — vocabulary for classifying stages.**
The model chunks + MC are Restricted use; the language note is
Clarification; "Your turn" approximates Authentic use (adapted: solo,
bounded by the frame).

## 8. Council of Europe — CEFR Action-Oriented Approach

- Sources:
  - https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/the-action-oriented-approach
  - CEFR Companion Volume chapter (rm.coe.int/…/1680a97137)
- Retrieved: 2026-10-04 via official indexed page text (direct fetch 403)

What it says:

- The learner is a **social agent** who must accomplish real-life tasks;
  learning is organized around purposeful action, learner **agency**,
  and authentic materials; can-do descriptors orient goals toward what
  learners _can_ do (proficiency view) rather than gaps.
- Full action-oriented _scenarios_ span several lessons and culminate in
  collaborative tasks/artefacts.

Limits for Lesson 001:

- A single solo lesson cannot implement a full scenario or a
  collaborative task — we borrow the _orientation_ (real-world purpose,
  agency, can-do framing), not the full apparatus.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES — orienting principle only.** The desired
result is phrased as a real-world action, not a grammar point.

## 9. TESOL — Task-Teach-Task

- Source: TESOL Resource Center — "Task-Based Instruction: Task-Teach-Task
  Lesson Plan Template" (+ PDF)
- URL: https://www.tesol.org/resource-center/task-based-instruction-task-teach-task-lesson-plan-template/
- Retrieved: 2026-10-04

What it says:

- Task 1 can diagnose learner needs/existing knowledge; Teach targets
  the focal language revealed by Task 1; Task 2 reuses the language in a
  different communicative task. Tasks must convey meaning and serve a
  communicative purpose; the cycle typically spans a session or multiple
  lessons with a live teacher reacting to evidence.

Why it does NOT fit Lesson 001:

- Task 1 presumes a learner attempting the task so a teacher can
  diagnose and respond. An absolute beginner with no prior English
  cannot attempt "meet someone" cold, and a static app cannot adapt
  teaching to task performance. The diagnostic value is lost.
- Classified: **RESEARCHED, NOT SELECTED** — documented as a real
  alternative that fails this lesson's constraints.

## 10. Cambridge — CELTA Syllabus (Topic 4)

- Source: "CELTA Syllabus and Assessment Guidelines", Cambridge English
- URL: https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/images/21816-celta-syllabus.pdf
- Retrieved: direct PDF + text extraction, 2026-10-04

What it says (Topic 4.2, verbatim criteria):

- Plans include: a statement of aims; a class profile; anticipation of
  difficulties and suggested solutions; teacher/learner interactions;
  resources; "staged description of procedures including anticipated
  timings."
- Topic 4.4: select, adapt and evaluate materials "with due regard for
  the provisions of copyright."
- 4.1: choose lesson _types_ appropriate to particular learners.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES — quality-control checklist, not a
sequence.** Our spec mirrors it: aim, learner profile, staged
procedures, rights-checked adapted materials, anticipated difficulties.

## 11. Rosenshine — Principles of Instruction

- Source: B. Rosenshine, "Principles of Instruction", American Educator
  36(1), Spring 2012 (AFT)
- URL: https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/Rosenshine.pdf
- Retrieved: direct PDF + text extraction, 2026-10-04

What it says (the 10 principles, verified in text):

- Short review; present new material in **small steps** with practice
  after each; many questions/checking; **models**; **guided practice**;
  check understanding; high success rate; scaffolds; **independent
  practice**; weekly/monthly review.

Limits: general-education evidence (not ESL-specific); "review"
principles assume a course timeline beyond one lesson.

Applies to Lesson 001: **YES as supporting evidence** — small steps,
model before practice, guided → independent progression.

## What the sources collectively do NOT support

- Any **universal fixed lesson sequence**. BC's own ordering guidance is
  hedged ("very general rule"); ARC explicitly permits any order; TTT
  contradicts ARC's default; ESA is presented as an option.
- That listening must always come before language focus — it does for a
  _listening_ lesson, by the listening framework's own logic.
- That a solo app can claim interpersonal proficiency.
- That one successful attempt demonstrates mastery.
- That distractors, explanations or example sentences may be invented
  freely — nothing in pedagogy research licenses inventing language
  content; SE's own governance (§9 of the task brief) forbids it anyway.
