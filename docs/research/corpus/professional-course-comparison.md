Official unit goals ("IN THIS UNIT YOU LEARN HOW TO"):

```
describe people and places        introduce yourself
explain where you are from        talk about jobs and where you work
ask and answer common questions   say more about your town / city
+ VIDEO  + REVIEW  + WRITING (Forms, at back of book)
```

Unit-internal sections (per series design): opener photo →
natural dialogues → GRAMMAR → VOCABULARY → CONVERSATION PRACTICE →
REVIEW AND SPEAKING + Pronunciation-and-review work.

**Documented design choice (official introduction, verbatim basis):**
verb be in Unit 1 is practised through *question words, numbers and
family* as the main lexical sets — **deliberately not**
countries / nationalities / alphabet "which are common in many other
books", because numbers/names/people answers are simpler and the
exchanges recycle more naturally. Countries/nationalities are held in
a reference section (p.139).

## 3. Answers to the ten R1.2 questions

### Q1. Which structural patterns recur across professional courses?

Recurring in **all five**:

1. **Objective-titled lessons** — lessons are named by capability,
   not grammar ("Say your name and country", "Nice to meet you",
   "Checking in", "Hello", "introduce yourself").
2. **be-verb decomposition** — `be` split across 2–3 lessons by
   person set (I/you → he/she/it → we/they). Ordering varies; the
   decomposition does not.
3. **Pronunciation in every lesson** — never a separate appendix
   strand only; always tied to the lesson's language.
4. **A dedicated functional/survival strand inside the unit** —
   Empower 1C Everyday English; EF Practical English; Speakout 1C
   How-to; Roadmap English in Action; Outcomes conversation
   practice. Real-world transactional language is separated from
   the grammar-led lessons.
5. **Unit-end review/self-check** — Empower Unit Progress Test;
   EF Revise and Check; Speakout Unit Review; Roadmap Check and
   Reflect; Outcomes Review and speaking.
6. **Personal profile writing** — appears explicitly in Empower
   and Roadmap (identical framing: capital letters + full stops);
   EF Writing Bank and Outcomes Forms cover it at back.
7. **Embedded video** — all five ship video inside Unit 1
   (EF drama serial; Speakout BBC street interviews; Outcomes
   NatGeo; Roadmap video; Empower 2e video).
8. **Reference banks at the back** — Grammar/Vocabulary(/Sound/
   Mediation) Banks or Grammar Reference + Vocabulary Builder in
   every course.

Recurring in **most**:

- **countries** as a U1 lexical set: Empower, EF, Speakout, Roadmap
  (4/5 — Outcomes explicitly demotes it, see Q3).
- **numbers**: EF (0–10 in 1A), Outcomes (13–22 in U1), Roadmap
  (1–100 in U2 — delayed).
- **the alphabet**: EF (Practical English), Speakout (1C);
  Empower/Roadmap/Outcomes do not foreground it in U1.

### Q2. Which patterns are Cambridge-specific?

1. **Skills declared per lesson in the scope and sequence** —
   L/R/S/W columns on every lesson row (EF declares lesson
   content but not a per-lesson skills column; Speakout declares
   one lead skill per lesson; Roadmap isolates skills into their
   own lessons).
2. **Dual-channel assessment** — content-progress tests (2
   attempts, error→recommendation loop) vs CEFR-mapped competency
   tests (1 attempt) as separate official instruments.
3. **Getting Started warm-up spread** — an image-led pre-lesson
   opener inside the unit.
4. **Corpus/English-Profile-informed syllabus claim** as a
   marketing and design property.
5. **"Learn together to succeed" collaborative layer** — built
   around classroom co-presence.

### Q3. Where does each specialist differ materially from Cambridge?

- **English File**: back-matter *Banks* architecture carries half
  the teaching load (Grammar/Vocab/Sound/Mediation Banks +
  Communication + Writing Bank + Listening scripts); functional
  strand is a serialized drama (Pictures of you), not a lesson
  section; adds mediation and Skills/Exam-Confidence strands;
  Revise and Check spans every *two* Files, not one.
- **Speakout**: every lesson leads with a named *skill* (Listening
  / Reading / How-to / authentic video) rather than grammar;
  review concentrates on **sounds and spelling** as a system
  (syllables, /ə/, plural allomorphs); BBC-produced street
  interviews as input.
- **Roadmap**: skills are *separate lessons* interleaved with
  language lessons (main → Develop-your-skills alternating), plus
  a **dual route** (fast-track vs extended); every objective is
  a measurable **GSE score** — the only course that publishes
  numeric difficulty per objective; self-assessment via Check and
  Reflect, not a test.
- **Outcomes**: the most divergent. Goals are framed as
  communicative outcomes; grammar introduced **as phrases** and in
  **staggered subsets**; no short-auxiliary-answer practice in
  main units (deferred to Elementary U7); Unit 1 deliberately
  avoids countries/nationalities/alphabet; official L1 policy
  ("a definite yes" for initial meaning work); accuracy
  expectations explicitly low at Beginner; recycling engineered
  into unit architecture rather than appended.

### Q4. What is the observed lesson/unit practice depth?

- Empower 1C (1e measurement): ~24 exercise items, 7+ media, 3
  pronunciation focuses, writing task, progress gate — the
  deepest single-lesson envelope observed.
- Speakout 1A–1D: 4 lessons each with listen/read input +
  controlled practice + functional output; ~15–20 items/lesson
  band (page-level observation, PARTIAL).
- Roadmap U1: 8 lesson-pages worth (4 main + 3 DYS + EIA) — the
  most granular segmentation; each lesson has a single goal.
- EF File 1: 2 two-page lessons + PE episode + Workbook +
  Revise&Check — moderate page count but Banks add parallel depth.
- Outcomes U1: fewer exercise-count-dense pages; depth moved into
  conversation-practice cycles and review sections.

**Consistent finding:** a professional beginner unit = 3–8
lesson-spreads + functional strand + review + reference support.
The *unit* is the real depth unit; single lessons are shallow by
design except when they carry a skills focus.

### Q5. How do UNIT / LESSON / SECTION / SESSION boundaries differ?

| Course | UNIT | LESSON | SECTION | SESSION |
|--------|------|--------|---------|---------|
| Empower 2e | capability cluster, 3 lessons + test | objective-titled spread | grammar/vocab/pron/skills/comm blocks | undefined (teacher-paced) |
| EF 5e | "File" = 2 lessons + PE/R&C cadence | 2-page spread | activity sequence | undefined |
| Speakout 3e | 4 skill-led lessons + review | one lead skill + G/V/P | input → practice → output | undefined |
| Roadmap | alternating language + skills lessons | single-goal page | presentation → practice → goal | undefined |
| Outcomes | goal-list cluster | spread with grammar/vocab/conversation | dialogues → practice → conversation practice | undefined |

**None of the five defines a learner-facing session boundary.**
Session pacing is delegated to the teacher/class. This confirms
the ChatGPT decision-2 gap: the web product must invent
UNIT/LESSON/SECTION → SESSION mapping itself; there is no
professional-convention answer to copy.

### Q6. How is pronunciation integrated?

All five: per-lesson slot tied to lesson language, plus unit-end
pron review (Speakout systematic sounds-and-spelling; Outcomes
Pronunciation-and-review; EF Sound Bank + alphabet in PE;
Empower stress/intonation in 1C; Roadmap short forms of be +
comprehension-focus pron). Two schools visible:

- **Contrast/system school** (EF, Speakout): phoneme contrasts,
  spelling-sound mapping, syllable/ə/plurals as systems.
- **Functional/prosodic school** (Empower, Outcomes, Roadmap):
  stress, intonation, reduced forms needed for the lesson's
  exchanges.

### Q7. How much controlled vs personalized production exists?

Universal pattern: **controlled → guided → personalized**, with
the personalized stage at lesson or unit end ("introduce
yourself", "talk about people you know", photos-of-your-family
conversation practice, profile writing). Controlled work
dominates item count; personalization carries the goal. Outcomes
explicitly frames exercises as rehearsals for a target
conversation; Roadmap names the personalization as the "speaking
goal" up front.

### Q8. How is review handled?

- In-lesson: Empower Check/Review Your Progress.
- Unit-end: all five (test, review, or reflect format).
- Cross-unit: EF Revise and Check every 2 Files; Outcomes
  engineered recycling (openers, dialogues, Grammar reference
  pron work, Vocabulary Builder); Roadmap extended route re-runs
  skills lessons.
- Vocabulary recycling is a named design property in Outcomes
  and EF (Vocabulary Bank/Builder), an assessment property in
  Empower (recommendations after errors).

### Q9. How is assessment separated from lesson completion?

- **Cambridge**: sharpest split — Unit Progress Tests (content,
  repeatable) vs Competency Tests (skills, CEFR-mapped,
  single-attempt). Learning progress ≠ proficiency.
- **EF**: Revise and Check (self) + Quick/File tests + Entry,
  Progress, End-of-Course tests (graded, A/B versions).
- **Roadmap**: Check and Reflect (self-assessment) inside;
  assessment package external.
- **Speakout**: Unit Review inside; course tests external.
- **Outcomes**: no embedded test; ExamView for teacher-built
  tests — weakest formal in-book assessment.

Professional consensus: **in-book gates are formative/self-check;
graded or proficiency evidence lives outside the lesson flow.**
Cambridge is the only one with an explicit second proficiency
channel mapped to CEFR.

### Q10. Which classroom mechanics cannot transfer directly to solo web learning?

1. **Pair/group conversation practice** — every course's
   production stage assumes a live partner ("work in pairs",
   "show photos on your phone", "check names of other people in
   the class").
2. **Teacher monitoring and on-the-spot correction** — TB lesson
   plans (EF sample, Klett Empower) are scripts for circulating,
   eliciting, board work, error feeds.
3. **Differentiation by a human** — Outcomes' real-vs-false-
   beginner strategy and EF's "extra ideas" are teacher
   judgments, not algorithmic.
4. **Class mingle/information-gap tasks** — require multiple
   live interlocutors.
5. **Spoken drills with feedback** — listen-and-repeat exists
   everywhere but corrective feedback is the teacher's role.
6. **Homework/extension assignment** — Workbooks, extra
   photocopiables, "go online for the video" assume a teacher
   assigning and checking.

Transfer implication (evidence-level, not design): the courses
all externalize interactive production to humans. A solo-web
product must replace partner, monitor, and assigner with
something else (scripted interlocutors, self-recording with
guided comparison, agent dialogue) — there is no professional
convention to copy here; this is the design gap SE must close
itself.

## 4. Contradictions and open questions recorded

- **Countries/alphabet debate**: 4/5 courses put countries (and
  often alphabet) in Unit 1; Outcomes documents why it refuses
  (simpler answers, better recycling) and demotes both to
  reference. Not resolvable by research — a design value
  difference (transactional travel English vs maximal-exchange
  English). Record as contradiction, don't pick a side.
- **be-verb ordering**: I/you first (all) but plural forms land
  in different lessons/files; EF defers we/you/they to File 2,
  Empower puts we in 1A. Sequencing granularity is a course
  choice, not a convention.
- **Skills placement**: embedded per lesson (Empower), one lead
  skill per lesson (Speakout), separate interleaved lessons
  (Roadmap), back-of-book bank (EF Writing/Listening). No
  consensus.
- **Assessment depth**: Cambridge's dual-channel model has no
  equivalent elsewhere; EF has the densest test apparatus;
  Outcomes has almost none in-book.
- **Roadmap 2e**: R1.2 evidence is 1e (2021). A 2e exists in
  market listings; if the architecture changed, recheck —
  currently PARTIAL/legacy flag, same discipline as Empower 1e.
- **Outcomes 3e Beginner**: 3e confirmed at Upper-Intermediate
  (2024); Beginner 3e availability UNKNOWN — 2e evidence treated
  as legacy until verified.
- **Session boundary**: no professional course defines one —
  confirmed gap for SE to design.
