# R1.4A — SLA / Learning-Science Evidence Map

> Locked structure (ChatGPT R1.3 review): for every finding —
> **SOURCE EVIDENCE** (what the research says, with citation),
> **PRODUCT IMPLICATION** (what it would mean for SE if adopted —
> implication, not decision), **UNCERTAINTY** (limits, context
> dependence, dissent). Prefer systematic reviews / meta-analyses /
> landmark studies. Product implications are research outputs, not
> build decisions.

## 1. Retrieval practice / testing effect

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological
Science* — testing produced substantially greater retention than
restudying at delayed intervals, even without feedback;
restudying inflated confidence while underperforming. Dunlosky et
al. (2013), *Psych. Science in the Public Interest* — practice
testing rated **high utility**, the top-rated technique alongside
distributed practice, generalizing across conditions, materials
and ages.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — exercises/tests are the learning event,
not a measurement bolted on. Retrieval should be the default
interaction mode (recall before recognition where feasible);
self-check items are high-value; "I feel like I studied" is a
weak signal — attempt evidence matters.

**UNCERTAINTY** — most primary studies use declarative material
(word lists, prose facts); effect sizes shrink for complex
skills; feedback timing interacts (test-only vs test+feedback);
production-quality retrieval (saying a sentence aloud ≠ recalling
a fact) is under-evidenced.

## 2. Spacing / distributed practice

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer
(2006), *Psychological Bulletin* meta-analysis — 839 assessments,
317 experiments: spaced beats massed robustly; optimal
inter-study interval grows with target retention interval;
expanding intervals work. Dunlosky et al. (2013): distributed
practice = **high utility** second technique.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — SE needs a real review/due system:
content re-encountered across days, intervals tuned to retention
horizon. Session model must interleave review with new material,
not just append.

**UNCERTAINTY** — optimal ISI for *language* specifically (vs
verbal recall generally) has thinner L2 evidence; Nakata's
L2-vocab spacing studies suggest similar direction but schedule
details vary; individual-difference calibration is an open
engineering problem (→ FSRS in Track B).

## 3. Corrective feedback

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Lyster & Ranta (1997), *SSLA* — six feedback
types mapped; recasts dominated teacher behavior yet were least
effective at eliciting student-generated repair; prompts
(elicitation, metalinguistic cue, clarification request,
repetition) forced repair. Follow-up syntheses (Lyster, Saito &
Sato 2013) keep prompts ≥ recasts for acquisition, recasts fine
for immediate notice.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — when SE detects a wrong/mistaken answer,
prefer a *prompt* that makes the learner re-produce over silently
showing the correct answer. "Show answer" ≠ feedback.

**UNCERTAINTY** — classroom interaction evidence; solo-app
translation (e.g., can software elicitation feel like
negotiation?) untested in the cited studies; written vs oral
modality differences.

## 4. Explicit grammar instruction

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Norris & Ortega (2000), *Language Learning*
meta-analysis, 49 studies — focused L2 instruction yields large
effects; **explicit > implicit**; Focus on Form and Focus on
Forms both large; effects durable. Outcomes' phrase-first-then-
analyze approach (R1.2) is a design choice, not an evidence
contradiction — the meta supports explicit attention somewhere
in the sequence.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — explicit grammar explanation belongs in
SE lessons; it can be sequenced after exposure (guided
discovery) rather than before it — position is a design choice
within the evidence.

**UNCERTAINTY** — pre-1998 literature; outcome measures skew to
discrete-point tests; transfer to spontaneous production less
measured; beginner-specific dosage unknown.

## 5. Vocabulary acquisition

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Nation (2013, 2nd ed.), *Learning Vocabulary
in Another Language* — knowing a word = form, meaning, use across
receptive→productive continuum; deliberate word-card learning
strong; four-strand balance (meaning-focused input, output,
language-focused learning, fluency). Hulstijn & Laufer (2001) —
involvement load: retention rises with task-induced
need+search+evaluation (composition > fill-in > reading).

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — depth over count: a "known" word should
mean multiple aspects practiced, not a tap-pairing. Tasks with
higher involvement load (use-it-to-say-something) beat passive
review; word cards remain legitimate.

**UNCERTAINTY** — incidental-learning rate from single exposures
is low and variable (Nagy); needed repetition counts cited in the
literature (5–16+) vary wildly by word/measure.

## 6. Comprehensible input

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Krashen (1985+) — i+1 input hypothesis;
Long (1983+) — interaction hypothesis: negotiation makes input
comprehensible and draws attention to gaps. Nation (via Waring &
Nation excerpt, 9781107623026) — meaning-focused input requires
~**98% known-word coverage** for unassisted comprehension; below
that, learning from input stalls.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — input level control is a hard
constraint, not a nicety: SE content must be selected/measured
for coverage (→ NGSL as coverage signal + graded sources) before
being offered; unknown-word density is a first-class metric.

**UNCERTAINTY** — 98% threshold is for unassisted reading; with
support (glosses, audio, images) lower coverage works; the
threshold for *listening* is higher-still in some studies (~95–98%
of a different kind); Krashen's strong form (input alone
suffices) is not accepted — output/instruction also matter
(Norris & Ortega; Swain output hypothesis).

## 7. Extensive reading

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Nakanishi (2015), *TESOL Quarterly*
meta-analysis — d = 0.46 group contrasts / 0.71 pre-post.
Jeon & Day (2016), 49 studies — small-medium positive; adults >
children; **web-based stories > paper**; curriculum-integrated ER
strongest.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — graded reading is evidence-backed and
directly implementable: the Storybooks/VOA-L1/SE-Wikipedia pool
(TIER A/C) is a real capability channel, not filler. Web delivery
is not a compromise.

**UNCERTAINTY** — most ER studies run weeks-to-months with
motivated cohorts; absolute beginner A1 coverage of "pleasure
reading" is thin — graded story reading works at L1 level texts
only if coverage is controlled.

## 8. Listening progression

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Vandergrift & Tafaghodtari (2010),
*Language Learning* — metacognitive process-based listening
instruction (predict/plan → monitor → evaluate) significantly
beat same-texts-same-repetitions control; weaker listeners gained
most. Vandergrift & Goh (2012) book — metacognition is the
teachable lever in listening.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — listening tasks should scaffold process
(prediction before audio, comprehension check, targeted re-listen
with transcript) rather than just "play and quiz".

**UNCERTAINTY** — classroom FSL context; self-directed solo
listeners may need the scaffolding enforced by design since no
teacher paces it.

## 9. Captions / transcripts

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Montero Perez, Van Den Noortgate & Desmet
(2013), *System* meta-analysis, 18 studies — **large** caption
effects on both listening comprehension and vocabulary;
proficiency level did NOT moderate. Winke, Gass & Sydorenko
(2010) — caption-first generally best for aural vocab; learners
report using captions for attention/processing AND as crutch.
Vanderplank (2016) — long-term "effects with" captions still
under-researched (confirmatory cycle caveat).

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — transcript-on-demand is evidence-backed;
default-off-but-available (click to reveal, or first-view-no-
transcript second-view-with) preserves listening work while
keeping the accessibility/segmentation benefit. VOA's own lesson
design (watch → quiz → transcript read-aloud) already encodes
this.

**UNCERTAINTY** — optimal sequencing per language/L1 varies;
crutch risk is real (learners stop decoding audio); long-term
skill transfer evidence thin.

## 10. Pronunciation instruction

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Munro & Derwing (1995), *Language Learning*
+ Derwing & Munro (1997) — intelligibility, comprehensibility and
accentedness are **partially independent**; heavy accent can be
highly intelligible. Derwing & Munro (2005)/2020 retrospective —
the intelligibility principle displaced nativeness as the
teaching goal; prosody contributes disproportionately to
comprehensibility vs segmentals.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — target intelligibility/comprehensibility,
not accent removal; prioritize prosody (stress, rhythm, sentence
melody) — which is exactly what the professional courses attach
to lesson language (R1.2 Q6) and what Evergreen's component does.
Recording-and-comparing is a legitimate practice loop.

**UNCERTAINTY** — which specific beginner items most limit
intelligibility is accent-group dependent; self-assessment of own
speech is unreliable → human/rubric or validated ASR needed for
any claim beyond practice.

## 11. Task-based learning

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Willis & Willis (2007); Ellis (2018) —
TBLT = task (meaningful outcome) → report → focus on form;
evidence (Norris & Ortega above) supports FonF inside meaningful
activity. Professional courses operationalize this as
"Speaking scenario"/"Real life" end-of-lesson performance
(R1.2).

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — SE lesson ends should be *do-something*
performances (say your intro, send a message), not quiz
checkpoints; grammar sections exist to serve the task.

**UNCERTAINTY** — solo digital "task" ≠ negotiated classroom
task; outcome authenticity is weaker alone; assessment of task
performance needs criteria, not self-attestation.

## 12. Writing feedback / revision

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Ferris & Roberts (2001) — focused,
selective feedback beats comprehensive error-marking; treatable
errors (rules) respond better than untreatable (lexical
choice). Kang & Han (2015), *Language Learning* meta-analysis —
written corrective feedback effective overall (g ≈ 0.5),
direct feedback strongest for discrete forms.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — SE writing tasks at A1 should target a
small declared error set (capitals, be-forms, word order) rather
than "everything"; feedback then forces a revision pass —
feedback→revise cycle like Write & Improve (LINK_ONLY benchmark).

**UNCERTAINTY** — beginner production is minimal; long-term
accuracy transfer debated (Truscott dissent, though minority).

## 13. Speaking practice

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Sato & Ballinger (eds., 2016) — peer
interaction produces modified output, feedback and negotiation;
effects mediated by task type and pairing. Interaction
hypothesis (Long) — output + negotiation drives noticing.
Gatbonton & Segalowitz — automatization via genuinely
communicative repetitive practice.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — solo SE can provide *practice
conditions* (models, prompts, record-replay-compare, fluency
timing) but **not interaction**: no claim of negotiated meaning.
This is the locked R1.1 decision now grounded: practice evidence
≠ interpersonal competence.

**UNCERTAINTY** — how much of interaction's benefit transfers to
self-talk/shadowing is genuinely under-measured; ASR-scored
conversations are emerging tech, not evidence.

## 14. Mastery / retention claims

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Bloom (1968, 1984) — mastery learning
(mastery threshold before proceeding + corrective re-loop) large
effects, most robust in structured sequential material.
Commercial products exploit "mastery" loosely (Duolingo crown
levels, Busuu fluency %).

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — per-item mastery states (not per-screen
completion) are the honest unit of progress; "completed" screens
must not masquerade as "mastered" items — ties to the locked
FORMATIVE ≠ PROFICIENCY split.

**UNCERTAINTY** — 2-sigma claims were tutoring-context;
criterion-referenced mastery thresholds for *skills* are
hard to operationalize; SRS "mastery" = retention prediction,
not capability.

## 15. Interleaving

**SOURCE EVIDENCE** — Dunlosky et al. (2013) — interleaved
practice rated **moderate/low utility** (mixed results, mostly
math skills); contrast with spacing which is high.

**PRODUCT IMPLICATION** — do not build exotic interleaving claims;
mixing review of *previously taught* items into sessions is
recycling (well-supported by professional practice), which is
different from laboratory interleaving.

**UNCERTAINTY** — for grammar-type discrimination tasks
interleaving may help; thin L2 evidence.

## Cross-cutting frameworks

- **Nation's Four Strands** (meaning-focused input, meaning-
  focused output, language-focused learning, fluency
  development) — a defensible completeness check for any SE
  lesson composition: all four should appear across a unit.
- **ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines** + **CEFR Companion Volume**
  (Council of Europe 2020) — outcome descriptors for the
  proficiency channel; REFERENCE_ONLY sources.
- **British Council / TESOL practice guidance** — classroom
  technique source; not efficiency evidence.

## Uncertainties that are product-shaped (for ChatGPT)

1. Optimal review intervals for A1 English specifically — no
   definitive schedule; FSRS-style adaptive prediction is the
   engineering answer, not a literature answer.
2. How much solo "speaking practice" transfers to interaction —
   under-evidenced; keep claims minimal.
3. Coverage threshold for *listening* input vs reading — 98%
   rule is reading-derived; audio support changes it.
4. Whether a fixed 4-strand-per-lesson pattern is needed or
   strands can distribute across sessions — professional courses
   distribute them within units, not every screen.
