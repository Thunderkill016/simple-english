## SOURCE FACT — the framework

**Principles of Good Practice (Cambridge English, 2017)**
- "Fitness for purpose" is the balance of **VRIPQ**: Validity, Reliability,
  Impact, Practicality, Quality management.
- Validity decomposes: construct → cognitive (does the task make candidates
  think/perform like the real skill), context, scoring, criterion,
  consequential (does the test cause good learning).
- Aligned to The Cambridge Approach + ALTE Code of Practice.

**Learning Oriented Assessment (LOA)**
- Definition (Purpura, via ALTE materials): "the collection and
  interpretation of evidence about performance so that judgements can be made
  about further language development."
- System requirements per the same materials: quality of evidence, quality of
  feedback/modified instruction, learner-autonomy development, alignment of
  external measures with classroom assessment.

**Empower's instrument stack (CONFIRMED)**
| Instrument | Attempts | Maps to | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In-lesson "Check Your Progress" | n/a | taught items | self-check |
| Unit Progress Test | 2 | unit objectives | evidence of learning |
| Mid-/End-course Competency Test | 1 | CEFR | evidence of proficiency |
| Write & Improve / Speak & Improve | free retries | CEFR-scaled *estimate* | **formative practice feedback — NOT proficiency evidence** (see correction below) |

- Post-test loop (customer story): review answers → platform suggests
  specific content to review → re-test later.
- "Personalised CEFR test report forms" ship with the digital pack.

## Answering the mission's questions

**What does Cambridge consider evidence of learning?**
Correct performance on items tied to what was taught — unit objective items,
two chances, classroom-adjacent stakes.

**What is evidence of proficiency?**
CEFR-mapped competency tests (and externally, full-QA Cambridge exams) —
independent of the taught sequence. NOT the free practice tools: those give
formative estimates only (Correction C).

**How is feedback generated / when is reassessment used?**
Score → review-answer stage → platform-recommended content → re-attempt.
Reassessment exists *for learning instruments* (2 attempts), withheld for
proficiency instruments (1 attempt) — because the evidence question differs.

**How does assessment influence next learning?**
Via the review-suggestion loop inside Cambridge One; the teacher also gets
gradebook data to "adapt their lesson accordingly" (frontmatter).

**How are validity/reliability/practicality handled?**
VRIPQ is the explicit design trade-off; low-stakes classroom tools optimise
for impact+practicality, high-stakes exams for reliability+validity.

## DEVIN ANALYSIS

1. Cambridge runs **two parallel evidence channels** — didactic (what was
   taught) and psychometric (what transfers). SE currently has only the first.
2. Feedback is a *route*, not a message: wrong → specific review content →
   retest. "Not quite — try again" is the seed of the same loop.
3. Attempt policy is a design signal, not friction: allow retries where the
   goal is learning; forbid them where the goal is measurement.
4. ~~Write&Improve/Speak&Improve prove Cambridge trusts machine-scored
   feedback as proficiency evidence~~ → **CHATGPT CORRECTION C (2026-10-04):**
   Cambridge's own help pages state Speak & Improve gives only an *automatic
   estimate*, is for research/practice, is **not** a Cambridge English exam,
   and does not go through the full QA of high-stakes assessment
   (`help.writeandimprove.com/en/articles/4397300`). Write & Improve likewise
   = submit → feedback → revise loop (`cambridgeenglish.org/vn/learning-english/
   write-and-improve/`). Reclassified: **FORMATIVE / PRACTICE ESTIMATE**, not
   validated proficiency certification. What they actually prove: Cambridge
   trusts machine scoring *for practice loops* — exactly the tier where a
   self-study product may use it, with honest labeling.
