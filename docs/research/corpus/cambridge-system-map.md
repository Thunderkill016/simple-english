# Cambridge System Map — R1.1

How the Cambridge English learning system works, reconstructed from official
sources. Scope: what SE can learn from the *system*, not the copyrighted content.

Sources (see `source-registry.csv` for full provenance): Empower 2e frontmatter +
integrated-learning brochure, Cambridge One help center + customer stories,
Principles of Good Practice (2017), ALTE LOA session slides, EGP/EP programme
literature, CELTA syllabus (2024), Klett Empower Starter reproduction,
Speakout/EF/NGL samples for contrast.

---

## 1. What decides WHAT learners study?

**SOURCE FACT**
- Empower's syllabus is explicitly "informed by English Profile and the
  Cambridge English Corpus" (integrated-learning brochure).
- English Profile produces Reference Level Descriptions: EGP = 1,200+
  corpus-derived grammar competency statements per CEFR level; EVP = words
  learners know per level (Harrison & Barker 2015; O'Keeffe & Mark 2017).
- CEFR supplies the level grid; Empower exists at 7 bands (Starter, A1–C1).

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
Cambridge separates three questions most products conflate: (a) what English
exists (native corpus), (b) what learners can actually do at each level
(learner corpus → EGP/EVP), (c) what to teach (syllabus). The syllabus is the
*intersection decision*, not a source of facts.

## 2. How does Cambridge determine LEVEL?

**SOURCE FACT**
- CEFR six levels + Pre-A1 (Starters/Movers/Flyers → Key → Preliminary →
  First → Advanced). Empower maps every unit and test to CEFR.
- Mid-/End-of-course Competency Tests "are mapped to the CEFR" and produce
  "personalised CEFR test report forms" (Cambridge One walkthrough, frontmatter).
- Write&Improve / Speak&Improve return CEFR-scale grades.

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
One ruler (CEFR) is used for three distinct purposes: placement, progress,
proficiency. Cambridge does not blur them — a Unit Progress Test result is
progress evidence, a Competency Test result is proficiency evidence.

## 3. How does Cambridge design lessons?

**SOURCE FACT (Empower Starter Unit 1, Klett reproduction — CONFIRMED)**

Each unit = lettered lessons (1A/1B/1C, and 1D in the A1 unit) + review:
- 1A "Learn to say your name and country" — G: be I/you/we; V: countries.
- 1B "Learn to talk about people you know" — G: be he/she/they; V: nationalities.
- 1C "Everyday English: Learn to meet and greet new people" — Useful Language
  (greetings + responses), 3-part video/listening story, P: tone,
  S: group practice, W: a personal profile + Writing Plus.
- Each 1C lesson pattern: Getting Started → input (watch/listen, predict →
  check → detail) → Useful Language → Pronunciation → Speaking (own names) →
  Writing → Check Your Progress → Unit Progress Test + Review Your Progress.
- Grammar/Vocabulary/Writing "Focus/Bank/Plus" pages sit at the back of the
  book — the lesson points at them ("Now go to Grammar Focus 1A Part 1 p.114").

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
A Cambridge "lesson" is an objective-titled skills chain, not a topic.
The objective ("meet and greet new people") is the unit of design; grammar and
vocab are resources pulled in to serve it. Practice runs
model → controlled → personalized-own-name → freer group production, then
writing consolidates, then self-check + external test close the loop.

## 4. Vocabulary / 5. Grammar sequencing

**SOURCE FACT**
- Unit 1: countries (1A) → nationalities (1B) — recycled across lessons.
- be spreads across three lessons by person: I/you/we (1A) → he/she/they (1B) →
  functional use in greetings (1C).
- EVP/EGP supply the *level* evidence for what appears when.

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
Grammar is staged by learner-need evidence, not by paradigm completeness —
be is deliberately fragmented across lessons rather than taught as one table.

## 6. Pronunciation

**SOURCE FACT**
- Every lesson has a labeled P block. In 1C alone: syllable count in greetings,
  stressed syllables, main stress in "I'm/My name's", tone change vs same.
- Klett notes show "Sound and spelling" (long/short sounds) inside 1A.

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
Pronunciation is micro-scoped and attached to the lesson's functional
language — it never appears as an isolated phonics unit in the lesson chain.

## 7–10. Skills treatment

**SOURCE FACT**
- Listening: predict → gist → detail → use. 1C has three video parts each with
  pre-, while-, and post-tasks (watch Part 1 and check prediction; again and
  complete sentences).
- Speaking: always with own names/real context, in pairs→groups; controlled
  frames precede free practice; Communication activities at back of book.
- Reading: functional and short (a profile, chat messages), serving the model.
- Writing: one concrete product per unit (personal profile; chat message),
  with a sub-skill focus (capital letters, full stops).

## 11–12. Assessment of learning vs proficiency

**SOURCE FACT**
- Three layers: in-lesson self-check ("Check Your Progress"), Unit Progress
  Tests (2 attempts allowed), Mid/End Competency Tests (1 attempt, CEFR-mapped
  report forms) (Cambridge One help + customer story).
- After a test, learners "review their answers … follow the platform
  suggestions to review specific content and later re-test" (Patricia story).
- PoGP: fitness for purpose = VRIP(Q) balance — different stakes get
  different instruments.

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
Cambridge distinguishes *evidence of learning* (can do now vs taught items)
from *evidence of proficiency* (CEFR-banded generalization). Attempt counts
differ because the instruments' purposes differ. Feedback is routed:
score → targeted review content → retest.

## 13. Corpora and learner errors

**SOURCE FACT**
- Cambridge Learner Corpus = 55M+ words of real exam scripts across 140+ L1s;
  EGP was built by tracking what learners *actually produce* at each level.
- EGP papers note "mismatch between ELT syllabi and competence" found in data.

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
Learner-error evidence flows corpus → profiles → syllabus decisions. Errors
are not just corrected; they *re-sequence* what is taught when.

## 14. Digital vs book

**SOURCE FACT**
- Digital Workbook (Cambridge One) carries Reading+Writing consolidation with
  audio; class video/audio live there; tests can be print or online;
  teacher gets gradebook + per-student data.
- Book lessons remain pair/group-social; the digital layer is practice,
  consolidation, and measurement — not a page replica.

## 15. Completion vs competence

**SOURCE FACT**
- "Review Your Progress" is a learner self-assessment grid; Unit Progress
  Tests exist separately; Competency Tests are CEFR-referenced.
- Speak&Improve/W&I exist *outside* the course as proficiency feedback.

**DEVIN ANALYSIS**
"Finished the lesson" and "can do the thing" are tracked on different
instruments — Cambridge never lets completion stand in for competence.
