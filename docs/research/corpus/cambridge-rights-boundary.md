# Cambridge Rights Boundary — R1.1

Mission §9. What R1 may and may not do with Cambridge material.

## Allowed (done)

- Inspected legitimate public samples: Klett distributor reproduction,
  Oxico excerpt, frontmatter/product PDFs, Cambridge One help pages,
  Principles of Good Practice, CELTA syllabus, free tools.
- Downloaded temporarily to `research_cache/cambridge/` (gitignored) for
  analysis.
- Extracted **abstract structure**: lesson skeletons, section names,
  exercise *types*, counts, sequencing patterns, assessment policy.
- Recorded bibliographic metadata, URLs, retrieval dates, page/section notes.

## Forbidden (not done — hard boundary)

- Committing Cambridge books, audio, video, copied exercises, or verbatim
  learner-facing English from Cambridge pages to the repository.
- Reusing Cambridge exercise content, scripts, or media inside SE lessons.
- Reproducing EGP/EVP statement lists wholesale (non-commercial terms; also
  verbatim corpus data).

## Gray zone → policy chosen

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Exercise-type labels ("Useful Language", "Getting Started", "Check Your Progress") | Structural concepts — citeable as fact; SE uses its own section names unless ChatGPT decides otherwise |
| EGP single statements in analysis docs | Individual "can" statements quoted for discussion = fair reference; bulk copy = no |
| Empower unit objectives as *facts* ("learn to meet and greet") | Facts about the source — citeable |
| Cambridge Corpus data of any kind | PROHIBITED — license required |

## Standing rule for future rounds

If a source page is copyrighted, the corpus doc gets: structure + counts +
source citations — never the teachable text itself. Any future exception
requires an explicit license, recorded in `source-registry.csv` before use.
